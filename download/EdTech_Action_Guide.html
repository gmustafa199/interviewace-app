How to Use This Guide

This is the simple version. No jargon. No fluff. Just clear steps and exact words to say to your AI assistant.

If you've read The Billion-Dollar EdTech Blueprint, you already know the strategy. This guide shows you how to actually do it — step by step, week by week, with prompts you can copy and paste.

The Three Rules

  • One step at a time. Don't skip ahead. Each step builds on the last.
  • Use the prompts exactly as written. Replace the bracketed text in [blue] with your own info, then paste into your AI chat.
  • Ask follow-up questions. If something doesn't make sense, ask your AI: "Explain this to me like I'm 12."

Your Starter Kit

  • An AI chat tool (this one works perfectly)
  • A notebook or notes app for your answers
  • 30 minutes a day, 5 days a week

01
Step One
Lock Your Thesis
Week 1–2
~5 hours
Your Goal
Write one clear sentence that says who you teach, what problem you solve, and what they get.

If you can't explain your company in one sentence, you don't have a company yet — you have a vibe. This step kills the vibe and forces clarity.

What to Prepare (10 minutes)

Before You Start
  • Open a blank document
  • Write down 3 groups of people you know well (e.g., "college students studying CS," "moms returning to work," "real estate agents")
  • Pick the one you care about most
  • Write 5 problems that group complains about

Copy-Paste This Prompt

→ Paste this into your AI chat
Edit the [blue] parts
I'm building an EdTech company. Help me lock my thesis. Here's what I know: - The learners I want to serve: [describe them — e.g., "working parents who want to switch into tech careers"] - The problem I think they have: [describe it in their words — e.g., "they don't have time for a 6-month bootcamp"] - What I think would help: [your rough idea — e.g., "30-minute daily AI tutoring sessions"] Please: 1. Rewrite my thesis as one clear sentence using this format: "We help [specific learner] go from [current state] to [desired state] by [your method]." 2. Tell me 3 reasons this thesis might fail (be honest). 3. Suggest 2 alternative theses I should consider. 4. List 5 questions I should answer before moving on.

What You'll Get Back

From Your AI
  • A one-sentence thesis you can put on your website
  • Honest reasons your idea might not work
  • 2 backup directions if your first idea is weak
  • 5 questions to think about before Step 2

Your Next Move

→ Do This Now
Save your final thesis sentence somewhere you'll see it daily. Then answer the 5 questions the AI gave you. Don't move to Step 2 until you can recite your thesis from memory.
If You're Stuck

If you can't pick a learner, ask yourself: "Who do I naturally help already?" The best EdTech founders start by serving people they already understand. You don't need to be an expert — you need to care.

02
Step Two
Validate Your Market
Week 3–4
~15 hours
Your Goal
Talk to 20 real people from your target group. Get at least 5 to say "I'd pay for this."

This is the step most founders skip — and it's the step that kills most startups. You need to know real people will pay real money before you build anything.

What to Prepare (30 minutes)

Before You Start
  • Write down your thesis sentence from Step 1
  • List 5 places your target learners hang out online (Facebook groups, Reddit, LinkedIn, Discord, etc.)
  • List 5 places they hang out offline (workplaces, schools, meetups)
  • Set up a free Calendly link for 20-minute chats
  • Prepare a simple Google Form for backup responses

Copy-Paste This Prompt — Part A

→ Build your interview script
Edit the [blue] parts
Help me write a 20-minute customer interview script. My thesis: [paste your thesis sentence from Step 1] My target learner: [describe them in detail] I need a script that: 1. Opens with a friendly 2-minute intro (no selling) 2. Asks 8 questions to understand their CURRENT problem (not pitch my idea) 3. Tests if they'd pay — without me asking "would you pay?" 4. Ends with asking for 2 referrals to other people like them Make the questions sound natural and conversational, not like a survey. Include follow-up prompts for each question so I know what to dig into.

Copy-Paste This Prompt — Part B (After Interviews)

→ After you've done 20 interviews
Use this to find patterns
I just finished 20 customer interviews for my EdTech company. My thesis was: [paste thesis] Here are my raw notes from the interviews: [paste all your interview notes — bullet points are fine] Please: 1. Tell me the top 3 patterns you see in what people actually want 2. Tell me where my thesis was wrong (be brutal) 3. Tell me how many of the 20 said they'd pay — and if that's enough to proceed 4. Give me a revised thesis based on what I learned 5. List 5 features my MVP MUST have based on what people said

What You'll Get Back

From Your AI
  • A ready-to-use interview script
  • Patterns from your 20 interviews
  • A revised thesis based on real data
  • A clear list of must-have MVP features

Your Next Move

→ Do This Now
Book 20 interviews. Do them in 2 weeks. Don't build anything until 5+ people have said "I'd pay for this" — and given you a price they'd pay.
The Magic Question

During interviews, never ask "Would you pay $50?" Instead ask: "What did you last pay to solve this problem?" If they've never paid for anything in this space, they won't pay you either.

03
Step Three
Build Your MVP with AI
Month 2–3
~60 hours
Your Goal
A working product you can charge money for. No coding required — your AI does the heavy lifting.

Your MVP (Minimum Viable Product) is the smallest version of your idea that solves the problem. It doesn't need to be fancy. It needs to work and accept payments.

What to Prepare (1 hour)

Before You Start
  • Your revised thesis from Step 2
  • The 5 must-have features from Step 2
  • A name for your product (use a free name generator if stuck)
  • $20 for a domain name (Namecheap or Google Domains)
  • A Stripe account (free to set up)
  • A list of 3 "no-code" tools you're willing to try

Copy-Paste This Prompt — Part A (Plan It)

→ Plan your build
Edit the [blue] parts
I need to build an MVP for my EdTech company. I'm not a developer and I don't want to hire one yet. My thesis: [paste thesis] My 5 must-have features: [paste features from Step 2] My budget: [$100 / $500 / $1000 — be honest] My time: [e.g., "10 hours per week for 6 weeks"] Please: 1. Recommend the simplest no-code stack to build this (tools like Bubble, Glide, Softr, Airtable, Make, Zapier, etc.) 2. Tell me exactly which tool does what 3. Give me a week-by-week build plan for 6 weeks 4. List the 3 things that will be hardest and how to handle each 5. Tell me what to leave OUT of the MVP (so I don't over-build)

Copy-Paste This Prompt — Part B (Build It)

→ Each build session
Repeat weekly
I'm on week [1/2/3/4/5/6] of building my EdTech MVP. My plan for this week is to: [paste what you planned to do this week] I'm using these tools: [list tools] So far I've done: [list what's done] I'm stuck on: [describe the problem in plain English — even "I don't know how to start"] Please: 1. Give me step-by-step instructions to finish this week's tasks 2. Use plain English — assume I've never used these tools before 3. If I should use a specific button or menu, tell me exactly where to click 4. If there's a video tutorial I should watch, tell me what to search for 5. Give me ONE clear next action to take right now

What You'll Get Back

From Your AI
  • A custom no-code stack recommendation
  • A 6-week build plan you can actually follow
  • Step-by-step weekly instructions
  • Plain-English help with specific tools

Your Next Move

→ Do This Now
Buy your domain today. Set up Stripe today. Pick your no-code tools today. Have a working URL you can send to a friend by end of Week 2. Ugly is fine. Broken links are not.
The MVP Rule

If your MVP took more than 6 weeks to build, you built too much. Cut features until you can launch. You can always add later. You can't add to a product nobody uses.

04
Step Four
Launch to 100 Paying Users
Month 4–6
~100 hours
Your Goal
Get your first 100 paying customers. That's roughly $5,000 in monthly revenue.

This is where the real work starts. Your MVP exists. Now you need people to use it — and pay for it. The good news: your first 100 users will teach you more than any course or book ever could.

What to Prepare (2 hours)

Before You Start
  • Your live MVP URL
  • A Stripe payment link working
  • The 20 people you interviewed in Step 2 (yes, go back to them)
  • A list of 10 communities where your learners hang out
  • A pricing decision: $X/month or $Y one-time
  • A short "founder story" you can post publicly

Copy-Paste This Prompt — Part A (Launch Plan)

→ Build your launch plan
Edit the [blue] parts
My EdTech MVP is live. I need to get my first 100 paying customers in the next 90 days. My product: [describe it in 2 sentences] My price: [$X/month or $Y one-time] My target learner: [describe them] Where they hang out: [list communities, social platforms, offline places] My budget for marketing: [$0 / $500 / $2000] My strengths: [e.g., "good at writing," "comfortable on video," "have a small Twitter following"] Please: 1. Give me a 90-day launch plan broken into 2-week sprints 2. Tell me the 3 highest-impact channels for MY situation (not generic advice) 3. Write me a "founder story" post I can use on social media (200 words) 4. Give me 5 outreach messages I can send to my Step 2 interviewees 5. Tell me what to do in Week 1, Week 2, etc. — be specific

Copy-Paste This Prompt — Part B (Weekly Review)

→ Every Sunday night
Track what's working
Here's my week in review for my EdTech launch: Users I had at start of week: [number] Users I have now: [number] New paying customers this week: [number] Revenue this week: [$X] What I tried this week: [list every marketing thing you did] What worked: [list what brought in users] What didn't work: [list what flopped] My biggest insight: [one sentence] Please: 1. Tell me what to double down on next week 2. Tell me what to stop doing 3. Suggest ONE new experiment to try 4. Tell me if I'm on track for 100 users in 90 days 5. Give me my top 3 priorities for next week

What You'll Get Back

From Your AI
  • A custom 90-day launch plan
  • A ready-to-post founder story
  • Outreach messages you can copy-paste
  • Weekly coaching based on your real numbers

Your Next Move

→ Do This Now
Email all 20 people from Step 2 today. Don't pitch. Just say "It's live — would love your honest feedback." Half won't reply. The other half will give you your first 10 customers.
The Brutal Truth

Your first 100 users will come from conversations, not ads. If you're not talking to 5 new people every day, you're not launching — you're waiting. Get on calls. Send DMs. Show up in communities. Be a person, not a brand.

05
Step Five
Scale to 10,000 Users
Month 7–12
~600 hours
Your Goal
Grow from 100 to 10,000 users. Find channels that scale without your direct time.

100 users came from your sweat. 10,000 users comes from systems. You need repeatable channels that bring in users while you sleep — content, partnerships, paid ads, or community.

What to Prepare (3 hours)

Before You Start
  • Your first 100 users' data — how they found you
  • Which channel brought the BEST users (highest retention)
  • Your current monthly revenue and burn rate
  • 3 pieces of content you've already made (any format)
  • A list of 20 potential partners in your space
  • 3 months of runway (cash in bank)

Copy-Paste This Prompt

→ Build your scale plan
Edit the [blue] parts
I have 100 paying users for my EdTech product. I want to scale to 10,000 users in the next 6 months. Current state: - Product: [describe] - Monthly revenue: [$X] - Monthly burn: [$Y] - How my best users found me: [describe] - My worst channel: [describe what flopped] - My team: [just me / me + 1 contractor / me + co-founder] - Cash in bank: [$X — 6 months of runway?] Please: 1. Pick the 2 channels I should go all-in on (based on what's working) 2. Give me a 6-month plan with monthly milestones 3. Tell me what to OUTSOURCE first (so I'm not the bottleneck) 4. Build me a content calendar for the next 30 days (10 pieces of content) 5. Tell me what partnerships to pursue and how to pitch them 6. Tell me when I'm ready to start paid ads (and on what platform) 7. Give me 5 metrics to track weekly that aren't vanity metrics

What You'll Get Back

From Your AI
  • A focused scale plan on 2 channels (not 10)
  • Monthly milestones you can measure
  • A 30-day content calendar
  • Partnership pitch templates
  • The right metrics to track

Your Next Move

→ Do This Now
Pick ONE channel from the AI's recommendation. Commit to it for 90 days. Don't try 5 channels at once — that's how founders burn out without results. Focus wins. Spread loses.
The Scaling Trap

Most founders try to scale too early. If your weekly active retention isn't above 60% at 100 users, scaling to 10,000 will just bring you 10,000 churned users. Fix retention first. Then pour fuel on the fire.

06
Step Six
Raise Your Round
Month 10–15
~200 hours
Your Goal
Raise $1M–$5M from investors to fuel your next phase of growth.

You don't need to raise money. But if you want to build a billion-dollar company, you probably will. This step shows you how to do it without giving away your company or your sanity.

What to Prepare (1 week)

Before You Start
  • 12 months of clean financial data (revenue, churn, CAC, LTV)
  • A clear story: why now, why you, why this market
  • A list of 50 investors who invest in EdTech at your stage
  • Warm introductions to at least 10 of them (LinkedIn, advisors)
  • 3 customers willing to take investor reference calls
  • A 10-slide pitch deck (your AI will help you write this)

Copy-Paste This Prompt

→ Build your raise kit
Edit the [blue] parts
I'm raising a seed round for my EdTech company. Help me prepare. My numbers: - Monthly revenue: [$X] - Monthly growth rate: [X%] - Users: [X total, Y paying] - Gross margin: [X%] - CAC (cost to acquire a customer): [$X] - LTV (lifetime value): [$X] - Cash in bank: [$X] - Runway left: [X months] My story: - Why I started this: [2-3 sentences] - Why I'm the right founder: [2-3 sentences] - Why now: [2-3 sentences] I want to raise: [$X] at [$Y valuation cap or pre-money] Please: 1. Write me a 10-slide pitch deck outline (slide title + key points for each) 2. Write a 100-word "teaser" I can send in cold emails to investors 3. Give me 5 likely investor objections and how to handle each 4. Tell me what documents I need to have ready before pitching 5. Write me a follow-up email template for after a pitch meeting 6. Tell me 3 red flags that mean I should NOT raise yet

What You'll Get Back

From Your AI
  • A complete pitch deck outline
  • Cold email and follow-up templates
  • Objection-handling scripts
  • A readiness checklist
  • Honest signals on whether you're ready

Your Next Move

→ Do This Now
Don't pitch investors until you have 3 warm intros booked. Cold outreach to investors has a 1% reply rate. Warm intros have a 60% reply rate. Spend a week getting intros before you send a single pitch.
The Honest Truth

If your monthly revenue is under $10K and growth is under 15% per month, you're not ready to raise a priced round. Consider a SAFE note or angel check instead. Don't waste 6 months pitching when you should be growing.

07
Step Seven
Build Your Operating System
Month 13–24
Ongoing
Your Goal
Stop being the bottleneck. Build systems, hire your first team, and become a CEO — not a busy founder.

At 10,000 users, you can't do it all yourself anymore. This step is about replacing yourself with systems and people — so the company grows even when you take a week off.

What to Prepare (2 hours)

Before You Start
  • A list of everything YOU do in a week (time audit)
  • The 3 things only YOU can do (your zone of genius)
  • The 10 things anyone could do (your delegation list)
  • Your revenue and profit numbers
  • Budget for first 2 hires

Copy-Paste This Prompt

→ Build your operating system
Edit the [blue] parts
I'm at 10,000 users and I need to build my operating system so I stop being the bottleneck. My time audit (what I do each week): [list everything — be honest, even "scrolling Twitter"] Things only I can do: [list 2-3 things] Things anyone could do: [list everything else] My team: [just me / me + contractors / me + 1-2 FT hires] My monthly revenue: [$X] My monthly profit: [$Y] Budget for hiring: [$Z/month] Please: 1. Tell me my first 2 hires (role + why) 2. Write job descriptions for each (200 words each) 3. Build me a weekly operating rhythm (what to do Mon, Tue, etc.) 4. Tell me which AI tools to use to automate each delegated task 5. Give me 5 KPIs I should track weekly as CEO (not operator) 6. Tell me what to STOP doing immediately 7. Write me a 90-day plan to transition from "doer" to "leader"

What You'll Get Back

From Your AI
  • Your first 2 hires with job descriptions
  • A weekly operating rhythm
  • An automation roadmap
  • CEO-level KPIs to track
  • A 90-day transition plan

Your Next Move

→ Do This Now
Do a time audit THIS WEEK. Track every hour for 7 days. You can't delegate what you can't see. Most founders are shocked at how much time they spend on $15/hour work.
The Identity Shift

This is the hardest step mentally. You built this company by doing everything. Now you have to let go of doing and start leading. The founders who can't make this shift stay stuck at $1M ARR forever. The ones who do become CEOs of billion-dollar companies.

When Things Go Wrong

Troubleshooting & FAQ

Real questions from real founders. If you're hitting a wall, the answer is probably here.

Q: I don't have an idea yet. Can I still start?
Yes. Use the Step 1 prompt but replace the [learner] and [problem] with "I don't know yet — help me discover them." The AI will guide you through a discovery process. Most founders start with a vague interest, not a fully-formed idea.
Q: I tried a prompt and the AI gave me generic advice. What did I do wrong?
You probably left the brackets blank or gave one-word answers. The quality of the AI's output depends on the quality of your input. Replace every [blue bracket] with 2-3 sentences of real detail. Vague in, vague out.
Q: I'm not technical. Will I really be able to build an MVP?
Yes. Modern no-code tools (Bubble, Glide, Softr, Make) let you build real products without code. Your AI assistant will give you step-by-step instructions. If you can use Excel and follow a recipe, you can build an MVP.
Q: How do I know if my idea is any good?
You don't — until Step 2. Talk to 20 real people. If 5+ say "I'd pay for this," your idea is good enough to build. If fewer than 5 say it, change something — either the learner, the problem, or the solution. The market tells you the truth.
Q: I'm stuck on Step 4. I built the MVP but no one's buying. Help.
Three likely causes: (1) You're not talking to enough people — aim for 5 new conversations a day. (2) Your price is wrong — try doubling it or halving it as an experiment. (3) Your MVP doesn't actually solve the problem — go back to your Step 2 interview notes and check.
Q: Do I really need to raise money? Can't I just bootstrap?
You can — and many billion-dollar companies are bootstrapped. Step 6 is optional. If you're profitable and growing, skip it. Only raise if you need fuel to grow faster than your profits allow. The Blueprint covers both paths.
Q: I'm overwhelmed. There's too much to do.
Use the "I'm Stuck" prompt from the Master Prompt Library. Then pick ONE thing — just one — and do it today. Momentum beats perfection. Most founders fail because they try to do 20 things at once instead of 1 thing well.
Q: How long will this whole thing take?
The blueprint is 24 months. But you'll see real progress in 90 days. By month 3, you'll have a live MVP. By month 6, paying customers. By month 12, real revenue. By month 24, a real company. The clock starts the day you run your first prompt.
Q: What if I fail?
Then you'll have learned more than 99% of people who never try. The skills you build in Steps 1-4 are worth more than the company itself. Most "failed" founders start their next company and succeed. The only real failure is never starting.
Final Word

This guide is a recipe, not a rulebook. Use it as your north star — but adjust based on what your market tells you. The founders who win aren't the smartest. They're the ones who keep going when it gets hard. Open Step 1. Copy the prompt. Start now. We'll figure out the rest together.
