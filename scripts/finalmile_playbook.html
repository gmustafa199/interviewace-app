Chapter 01 · The Commitment

What We Are Building Together

You asked for the real process, not the methodology. Here it is. We are building one specific product, from zero to public launch, over twenty weeks. I am committing to it as your senior full-stack engineer, your strategist, your operator, and your researcher. You are committing as the founder: the product owner, the customer voice, and the sole owner of content production. The previous document was the map; this document is the road.

The product is called FinalMile AI (working name — you can rename it anytime). It is a voice-first AI mock interview coach for Indian job seekers. The wedge — the narrow, painful, reachable entry point — is final-year engineering students preparing for IT/software placement interviews. India produces 1.5 million engineering graduates per year, and only 15 to 30 percent get placed through campus interviews. The interview is the single biggest source of fear, and the existing prep infrastructure (coaching classes, YouTube videos, generic question banks) does not solve the actual problem: students cannot practice answering questions out loud, under pressure, with realistic follow-ups, and get specific feedback on what they said wrong.

FinalMile AI solves this. A student opens the app, picks an interview type (TCS NQT, Infosys HackWithInfy, generic software developer HR round, Java technical round), puts on earphones, and has a fifteen-minute voice conversation with an AI interviewer that asks real questions, follows up based on their answers, and at the end produces a scorecard covering technical accuracy, communication clarity, confidence, and specific moments where they stumbled — with a recording of the session and recommended practice modules. The student can do this ten times a day for ₹499 per month, less than the cost of one hour with a human coach. That is the product. That is what we build.

Why This Wedge, Why Now
Three forces converge: (1) voice AI crossed the realism threshold in 2024 — a GPT-4o voice conversation now feels 85% as real as a human interviewer, up from 30% two years ago. (2) India's placement season is brutal and annual — every July to November, 1.5M freshers face interviews they are underprepared for, creating a predictable, recurring demand spike. (3) No Indian AI-first voice interview product dominates yet; the field is YouTube videos, PDF question banks, and offline coaching. First mover with a great product wins this category.
Chapter 02 · The Product Spec

FinalMile AI in Detail

Before any code, we agree on the spec. The spec is the contract between us: every feature listed here will be built by week 20; anything not listed here is explicitly out of scope for v1. This discipline is what separates a real product from a feature factory. Read this chapter twice — once now, once before we start week 5.

The Core Loop (what the user does every session)
  1. Pick interview type. Choose from a curated list (TCS NQT technical, Infosys SP coding, generic SDE HR round, Java backend technical, Python developer technical, data analyst SQL round, etc.).
  2. Brief context. Optional 30-second voice intro: name, college, target company, experience level. AI uses this to personalize questions.
  3. Conduct the interview. 10–15 minute voice conversation. AI asks 5–8 questions adapted to the chosen type, follows up based on answers, mimics real interviewer behavior (silences, nudges, "can you elaborate on that").
  4. Receive scorecard. 30-second processing, then a full report: overall score, sub-scores (technical, communication, confidence, structure), 3 strengths, 3 weaknesses, verbatim quotes from the session with annotations.
  5. Review recording. Full audio playback with timestamps for each question and each weakness flag.
  6. Practice recommendations. 3 specific practice modules based on weaknesses (e.g., "Practice 5 SQL joins questions", "Rehearse the 'tell me about yourself' answer 3 times with this template").
The Five Features v1 Must Ship
  • Voice interview engine. Real-time speech-to-text, GPT-4o for question generation and follow-ups, text-to-speech for AI voice. Latency under 2 seconds for natural conversation.
  • Interview type library. 12 interview types at launch, each with its own question bank and scoring rubric (you produce the question banks; I build the system).
  • Scorecard engine. Structured evaluation against per-interview-type rubrics, with quote-level annotations pulled from the transcript.
  • User dashboard. History of past interviews, score trends, weak areas heatmap, streak counter.
  • Payment + access control. ₹499/month or ₹2,999 placement season pass (August–December unlimited). Razorpay for India payments. Free tier: 1 interview per month.
The Fifteen Features v1 Will NOT Ship

Equally important. Each of these will be requested by users in beta. Each will be tempting to add. Each will be deferred to v2 or later. Print this list and pin it above your desk.

  • Live interviews with human coaches (marketplace feature, Phase 2)
  • Peer-to-peer mock interviews with other students (community feature, Phase 2)
  • Resume review or ATS optimization (separate product, never)
  • Linkedin profile optimization (never)
  • Coding environment inside the app (use existing platforms — LeetCode, HackerRank)
  • Video-based interviews (voice only for v1; video adds 10x complexity)
  • Multi-language interviews (English only for v1; add Hindi, Tamil, Telugu in v2)
  • Group discussions simulation (different product mechanics)
  • College-wise leaderboards (gamification trap, kills focus)
  • B2B college dashboard (Phase 3, after consumer traction)
  • Custom interview creation by user (Phase 2)
  • Integration with college placement portals (too slow, too fragmented)
  • Mobile native apps (responsive web app for v1; native apps in Phase 2)
  • Affiliate or referral program (Phase 2, after retention is proven)
  • AI-generated personalized study plans (Phase 2; v1 just recommends modules)

Chapter 05 · The 20-Week Plan

From Zero to Public Launch

Twenty weeks is the realistic timeline to a credible public launch with paying users. Not a perfect product — a real one, in the market, generating revenue, with retention data we can raise on. The plan is divided into four phases of five weeks each. Each phase has a single graduation metric; miss it and we do not advance, we iterate. This is not optional. Skipping phases to "go faster" is how founders ship broken products and lose six months fixing them.

Phase A · Weeks 1–5 · Foundation
Wedge validation, brand, landing page, waitlist
Goal: 20 user interviews completed, positioning statement finalized, landing page live, 300+ waitlist signups. Graduation: 30+ signups/week organically, at least 5 signups who explicitly say "I would pay for this."
Phase B · Weeks 6–10 · Core Build
Voice interview engine, scoring, dashboard, auth, payment
Goal: End-to-end working product on staging. A user can sign up, pick an interview type, conduct a voice interview, receive a scorecard, and pay. Graduation: I can complete a full interview and receive a useful scorecard myself, with no errors.
Phase C · Weeks 11–15 · Content & Beta
You produce v1 question banks; we run private beta with 50 users
Goal: 12 interview types live with your question banks, 50 paying beta users, weekly iteration cycle. Graduation: 30%+ week-4 retention, 5+ organic referrals, NPS above 30.
Phase D · Weeks 16–20 · Public Launch
Polish, performance, launch sequence, first $1K MRR
Goal: Public launch on Product Hunt, Hacker News, Reddit, LinkedIn. First $1,000 MRR. Graduation: We have a real business. Then we plan Phase 5: months 6–12 growth.

What "Content" Means in FinalMile AI
  • Question banks — 40–60 questions per interview type, organized by sub-topic and difficulty. Each question has the question text, expected topics to cover, follow-up strategy, and a difficulty level (easy/medium/hard).
  • Scoring rubrics — For each question type, what makes a 9/10 answer vs a 5/10 vs a 2/10. Used by the AI scorecard engine to evaluate.
  • Sample answers — 3 sample answers per question (strong, average, weak) with annotations on what's good and what's missing. Used to train the AI's judgment.
  • Practice modules — Short drills a user does after a weak interview. E.g., "SQL Joins Practice" = 10 join questions with explanations. ~30 modules total.
  • Marketing content — Weekly LinkedIn posts, YouTube shorts scripts, Twitter threads, blog essays. This is separate from product content and ongoing forever.
Suggested Production Workflow (your call to adapt)

You proved you can produce content at scale. The workflow below is a suggestion based on what's worked for similar content loads; modify freely. The key principle: produce in batches by interview type, not by content type. Don't write all 600 questions first then all rubrics — write all content for TCS NQT, then all content for Infosys SP, etc. This keeps context switching low and quality high.

  1. Pick one interview type per week. e.g., Week 9 = TCS NQT Technical.
  2. Research the actual interview pattern. Watch 5 YouTube videos of TCS NQT interviews, read 10 Glassdoor interview reports, talk to 3 friends who sat for TCS recently. Document the actual question patterns, not generic prep.

  4. Author the scoring rubric for each question type — a 1-page document per sub-topic describing what a 9/10 vs 5/10 vs 2/10 answer looks like.
  5. Author 3 sample answers per question (strong, average, weak) — these can be AI-assisted but should reflect real student answers, not textbook answers.
  6. Author 2 practice modules for the most common weaknesses in this interview type (e.g., "Coding Pattern: Two Pointers" + "HR: Tell Me About Yourself").
